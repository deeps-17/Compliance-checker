# DPDP Act 2023 Compliance Report

**Overall Compliance**: 43% - Non-Compliant

**Summary**: The privacy policy is heavily outdated and fails to comply with the mandatory provisions of the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023. It relies on the repealed Information Technology Rules 2011 framework, employs non-compliant 'deemed' and 'bundled' consent mechanisms, completely lacks mechanisms for children's data restrictions (such as prohibiting targeted tracking), does not disclose cross-border data flows, and fails to acknowledge statutory rights like data principal access, correction, or nomination.

---

### 🟡 Consent — Partial (Section 6)
**Assessment**:
The policy utilizes bundled, implied, and deemed consent (stating that merely accessing the platform or continuing to use it after policy updates signifies consent). Under Section 6 of the DPDP Act, consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous, requiring a clear affirmative action. Furthermore, withdrawal of consent is made difficult (requiring contacting a Grievance Officer) rather than being as easy as giving consent.

**Evidence**:
*By accessing or using Our Services and/or registering for an account with the Company, you expressly agree to be bound by the terms and conditions of this Privacy Policy and you are consenting to the Company's collection... This Policy may change from time to time, your continued use of the Company's Services after it makes any change is deemed to be acceptance of those changes*
 
---

### 🟡 Notice — Partial (Section 5)
**Assessment**:
While the policy details the categories of data collected and purposes of processing, it does not precede the consent request with a compliant Section 5 notice. It fails to provide information on how data principals can exercise their right to withdraw consent or register grievances, fails to explain how to make a complaint to the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI), and does not offer notice options in languages specified under the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution of India.

**Evidence**:
*We use the information We collect from and about you for a variety of purposes... [No mention of DPBI complaint channels or translation options in local Indian languages]*

---

### 🟡 Data Principal Rights — Partial (Sections 11, 12, 13, and 14)
**Assessment**:
The privacy policy mentions account deletion requests, but it fails to recognize or provide mechanisms for other core rights of Data Principals under the DPDP Act, including the right to obtain a summary of processed personal data, the right to access the identities of all other Data Fiduciaries/Data Processors with whom the data has been shared, the right to correction/updating, and the right to nomination.

**Evidence**:
*A user may request for deletion of its personal information by raising a request with the Grievance Officer as per the provisions of Section 14 of this Privacy Policy. [Silence on rights to access sharing partners, request corrections, or designate a nominee]*

---

### 🟡 Data Fiduciary Obligations — Partial (Section 8)
**Assessment**:
Although the policy references security safeguards and data retention limits, it fails to outline its statutory obligations under Section 8. Specifically, it lacks any mention of its obligation to ensure completeness and accuracy of data if used for decision-making or sharing, and completely fails to mention its duty to notify the Data Protection Board of India and affected individuals in the event of a personal data breach.

**Evidence**:
*We ensure to maintain reasonable physical, electronic, and managerial procedures to safeguard... We reserve the right to retain your personal information... for a period no longer than is required... [No mention of data breach notification obligations or fiduciary data accuracy duties]*

---

### 🔴 Children's Data — Fail (Section 9)
**Assessment**:
The policy violates several strict prohibitions under Section 9. It allows minors under the age of 18 to use the services under parental 'supervision' instead of obtaining 'verifiable consent' from parents or guardians. Crucially, the policy explicitly states that it engages in tracking, behavioral monitoring, and targeted interest-based advertising, without prohibiting or restricting these activities when applied to children, which is explicitly banned under the DPDP Act.

**Evidence**:
*If you are under the age of 18 years then you must use the Services under the supervision of your parent, or legal guardian... Advertising companies may use device IDs to track your use of Our applications... We use your personal information to display interest-based ads...*

---

### 🟡 Grievance Officer — Partial (Section 13)
**Assessment**:
The contact details of a Grievance Officer (Murthy S.N) are published. However, the mechanism is set up and referenced under the outdated and superseded Information Technology Act 2000 and IT Rules 2011, rather than being aligned to the grievance redressal obligations under Section 13 of the DPDP Act 2023.

**Evidence**:
*In accordance with Information Technology Act, 2000 and the Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures And Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011, the name and contact details of the Grievance Officer are provided below...*

---

### 🔴 Cross-border Data Transfer — Fail (Section 16)
**Assessment**:
The policy is completely silent regarding the geographical location of data storage and fails to mention if personal data is transferred, processed, or hosted outside of India, ignoring the cross-border data transfer framework outlined in Section 16 of the DPDP Act.

**Evidence**:
*[The policy contains no information or clauses regarding cross-border data transfers, overseas hosting, or storage locations]*
