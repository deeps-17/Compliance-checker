# DPDP Act 2023 Compliance Report

**Overall Compliance**: 2/7 Pillars Fully Compliant (3 Partially Compliant, 2 Non-Compliant)

**Summary**: Urban Company's Privacy Policy is partially aligned with the DPDP Act 2023. It performs well regarding Grievance Redressal and Cross-border Transfers, but fails significantly on Consent (due to bundled/implied consent), Children's Data (completely silent), and has critical gaps in Notice (lacks regional language options and DPBI complaint details), Rights (lacks Nomination), and Obligations (no breach notification mechanism).

---

### 🔴 Consent — Fail (Section 6)
**Assessment**:
The policy utilizes bundled and implied consent ('By using the Services, you confirm that you... consent...'), which violates the DPDP Act's requirement for free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous consent obtained through clear affirmative action. Additionally, withdrawing consent is not comparable to giving it, requiring a manual email request.

**Evidence**:
*By using the Services, you confirm that you have read and agree to be bound by this Policy and consent to the processing activities described under this Policy.*

---

### 🟡 Notice — Partial (Section 5)
**Assessment**:
While the policy details the data collected and the purposes of processing, it fails to provide the option to access the notice in regional languages listed in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution. It also fails to specify the procedure for data principals to make a complaint to the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI).

**Evidence**:
*Sections 2, 3, and 4 detail collected data and purposes, but there is no mention of accessing the notice in regional languages or how to complain to the DPBI.*

---

### 🟡 Data Principal Rights — Partial (Sections 11, 12, 13, and 14)
**Assessment**:
The policy recognizes the rights of access, correction, erasure, and grievance redressal. However, it completely omits the Right to Nominate, which allows a data principal to nominate another individual to act on their behalf in the event of death or incapacity.

**Evidence**:
*Section 7(a) covers access and correction; Section 8 covers deletion/erasure; Section 15 covers grievance redressal. No mention of nomination rights exists.*

---

### 🟡 Data Fiduciary Obligations — Partial (Section 8)
**Assessment**:
The policy addresses data accuracy, security safeguards, and deletion upon withdrawal or purpose completion. However, it is entirely silent on the statutory obligation to notify the Data Protection Board of India and affected data principals in the event of a personal data breach.

**Evidence**:
*Section 10 outlines data security measures and Section 11 outlines data retention. There is no clause or procedure detailing personal data breach notifications.*

---

### 🔴 Children's Data — Fail (Section 9)
**Assessment**:
The privacy policy is completely silent regarding the processing of minors' personal data. It does not establish verifiable parental/guardian consent requirements, nor does it prohibit tracking, behavioral monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at children.

**Evidence**:
*There are no references to children, minors, age limits, parental consent, or child-related tracking prohibitions in the entire policy document.*

---

### 🟢 Grievance Officer — Pass (Section 13 and Section 8(9))
**Assessment**:
The policy fully complies with this obligation by publishing the explicit name, designation, email address, and physical address of its designated Grievance Officer.

### 🟢 Cross-border Data Transfer — Pass (Section 16)
**Assessment**:
The policy mentions that personal data may be transferred to and stored in countries outside India and explicitly commits to complying with applicable laws regarding storage and transfers of personal data.

**Evidence**:
*Section 9(a) and 9(b): 'We comply with applicable laws in respect of storage and transfers of personal data... the information and personal data you provide to us may be transferred to and stored in countries other than the country you are based in.'*
