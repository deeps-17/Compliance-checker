# DPDP Act 2023 Compliance Report

**Overall Compliance**: 2 out of 7 Pillars Passed (Critical Non-Compliance)

**Summary**: The organization's privacy policy shows critical gaps in compliance with India's DPDP Act 2023. While it successfully provides the details of a Grievance Officer and discloses cross-border storage practices, it fails on key foundational mandates. Specifically, it relies on invalid browse-wrap/implied consent, fails to establish verifiable parental consent mechanisms for processing children's data, omits statutory personal data breach notification requirements, and does not fully acknowledge mandatory Data Principal rights such as the Right to Access and the Right to Nominate. To align with the DPDP Act, the policy requires a complete overhaul to transition to affirmative, clear, and unbundled consent mechanisms, complete notices, and robust right-exercise channels.

---

### 🔴 Consent — Fail (Section 6(1), Section 6(4), Section 6(7))
**Assessment**:
Consent is implied merely by visiting or browsing the platform, which constitutes browse-wrap consent. Under Section 6(1) of the DPDP Act, consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous, requiring a clear affirmative action. Implied or bundled consent is legally invalid. Furthermore, the policy fails to mention the right to withdraw consent with the ease of doing so being comparable to the ease of giving it, nor does it mention Consent Managers.

**Evidence**:
*By visiting our Platform or by voluntarily providing your information, you consent to the collection, use, storage, disclosure and otherwise processing of your information on the Platform in accordance with this Privacy Policy.*

---

### 🟡 Notice — Partial (Section 5(1), Section 5(3))
**Assessment**:
While the policy details the types of data collected and broad purposes, it does not meet the strict notification standards of the DPDP Act. Specifically, it fails to outline the manner in which Data Principals may withdraw consent, the procedure to make a complaint to the Data Protection Board of India, or provide the option to access the notice in regional languages specified in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution.

**Evidence**:
*This privacy policy ('Privacy Policy') describes how Flipkart collects, uses, shares, stores or otherwise processes your personal information through the Flipkart Group Corporate website... Please contact us using the details below in case you want to exercise any 'Data Subject Rights' as per the applicable laws.*

---

### 🟡 Data Principal Rights — Partial (Section 11, Section 12, Section 14)
**Assessment**:
The policy broadly mentions accuracy, erasure, and 'Data Subject Rights'. However, it does not explicitly acknowledge the Right to Access, which under Section 11 must provide a summary of processed data and the identities of all other Data Fiduciaries/Processors with whom data has been shared. It also entirely omits the Right to Nominate an individual in the event of death or incapacity.

**Evidence**:
*We take every reasonable step to ensure that your personal information that we process is accurate and... erased or rectified. Please contact us... in case you want to exercise any 'Data Subject Rights' as per the applicable laws.*

---

### 🟡 Data Fiduciary Obligations — Partial (Section 8(5), Section 8(6), Section 8(7))
**Assessment**:
The policy details security safeguards and purpose-linked retention limits. However, it completely fails to declare its statutory obligation under Section 8(6) to notify the Data Protection Board of India and affected Data Principals in the event of a personal data breach. It also fails to specify that it will obligate its Data Processors to erase personal data upon purpose completion or consent withdrawal.

**Evidence**:
*We maintain reasonable physical, electronic and procedural safeguards to protect your information... We retain your personal information in accordance with applicable laws, for a period no longer than is required for the purpose for which it was collected...*

---

### 🔴 Children's Data — Fail (Section 9(1), Section 9(2), Section 9(3))
**Assessment**:
The DPDP Act strictly mandates obtaining verifiable parental or lawful guardian consent before processing any personal data of a child (under 18). Rather than executing a verifiable consent mechanism, the policy attempts to shift the legal burden onto the user by requiring them to 'represent' that they have authority. It also fails to explicitly prohibit tracking, behavioral monitoring, targeted advertising, or processing that is detrimental to child well-being.

**Evidence**:
*We do not knowingly solicit or collect personal information from children under the age of 18 years. If you have shared any personal information of children under the age of 18 years, you represent that you have the authority to do so and permit us to use the information in accordance with this Privacy Policy.*

---  

### 🟢 Grievance Officer — Pass (Section 8(9), Section 13(1))
**Assessment**:
The policy satisfies the statutory requirement of publishing the contact details of a designated Grievance Officer, sharing their name, postal address, and contact email.

**Evidence**:
*Grievance Officer: Shremanth M, Flipkart Internet Pvt. Ltd., Embassy Tech Village... Email: privacy.grievance@flipkart.com*

---

### 🟢 Cross-border Data Transfer — Pass (Section 16, Section 5(1)(i))
**Assessment**:
The policy states that the data will primarily be stored and processed within India. Under Section 16 of the DPDP Act, cross-border transfers are permitted unless specifically restricted or blacklisted by the Central Government, so this localized processing statement is legally sufficient.

**Evidence**:
*...in case you voluntarily share your personal information, it will primarily be stored and processed in India...*
